Anatomy

FooterA flex row under the dialog text, aligned to the end edge, 8px between buttons. Every button the same height and style family; only fill and color rank them.
Main actionThe one that does what the title asked. Solid primary, or solid destructive when the dialog is about deleting. Its label repeats the verb in the title.
Dismissing actionCancel, Keep editing, Close. Secondary or tertiary. “Cancel” stops and discards; “Close” changes nothing about the task (Fluent).
Third actionAn alternative outcome, such as “Don’t save”. It is tertiary, because it is a different answer, not the main one. More than three buttons belong in a different pattern: Fluent allows up to three, Carbon says more than three go in a menu.
StackUnder about 300px of footer width the row becomes a column with the main action on top and every button full width.

Where the main action goes

Platforms disagree, and the disagreement is documented. Follow the platform your users are on; if you do not know, choose one and use it everywhere (NN/g: platform convention matters more than optimizing one dialog).

Specs

Our own values. The systems’ own numbers are on their button pages; gaps shown here (8px) are Material’s and Fluent’s, Carbon uses 16px.

Behavior

LabelsName the outcome: “Delete file” and “Cancel”, not “OK” and “Cancel”. Fluent: in dialogs labels respond to the title; one or two words. Apple: avoid “Yes” and “No”; use one or two words; never explain the buttons with extra text. NN/g: use specific labels (Save, not OK).
DestructiveThe destructive button is red but still sits where the main action does. Apple: never give the primary role to a destructive action and always include Cancel. NN/g: restate the specific item, label by outcome, avoid a default “Yes”, and offer undo; for the worst cases require something deliberate such as typing a word (see Loading and Confirming Buttons).
Default focusFocus the main action unless it is destructive; then focus the safe one. A dialog with no inputs focuses its primary button (Carbon). Fluent returns focus to the trigger on close.
Visual order equals Tab orderThe markup order is dismissing then main. reading-flow: flex-visual on the flex container makes keyboard focus follow the visual order after row-reverse or column-reverse, so reversing never splits what you see from what Tab does.
Equal widthWithin a footer buttons share a height; Carbon also fixes the width of a group by its longest label. Here min-width keeps short labels from making a ragged pair.
StackA container query on the footer, not the window, so the same footer is correct in a modal, a side panel and a card.
WizardsCarbon’s progress modal puts Cancel (ghost) at the far left and Previous and Next grouped on the right. Fluent says Next and Previous, not Continue and Back.
Reduced motionButton transitions stop.

Accessibility

  • The dialog is named by its heading (aria-labelledby); in a real dialog, focus moves in on open and returns to the trigger on close.
  • Fluent’s alert dialog, used for destructive confirmation, is dismissed only by one of its footer buttons.
  • Tab order matches visual order in both conventions. Without reading-flow a reversed row would focus the dismissing button first on the left and the main action last on the left, the opposite of what is shown.
  • Never rely on color for the destructive meaning: the label names the action.
  • Buttons are 40px high with 8px between them, over WCAG 2.2’s 24px minimum.
