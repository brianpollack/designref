Anatomy

TreatmentsFour, from loudest to quietest. Prominent (filled): accent background, white label; SwiftUI .borderedProminent. Tinted: accent at low opacity behind an accent label. Gray (bordered): neutral fill, accent label. Plain: label only.
ShapesCapsule (radius above half the height) or rounded rectangle (12px here). Icon-only buttons are circles.
ContentAn icon, a label, or both. Labels use title-style capitalization and start with a verb (“Add to Cart”). Icons are Lucide here; the SF Symbols they stand in for are listed below.
Icon mapAdd: plus (Lucide plus). Delete: trash (trash-2). Cancel/Close: xmark (x). Done: checkmark (check). More: ellipsis (ellipsis). Share: square.and.arrow.up (share). Favorite: heart (heart).
RoleNormal, Primary, Cancel or Destructive. Primary is the filled button and answers the Return key. Destructive is system red. A destructive button is never the primary role, even when it is the likely choice.
Compact faceMini (28) and Small (32) draw a smaller face inside a 44px button, so the touch target stays 44. This is our technique; Apple lists the heights only for visionOS.
Toggle buttonA gray button with aria-pressed. On, it turns accent-filled and swaps the heart for a check, so the state is not carried by color alone.
Liquid GlassA translucent fill over a blurred, saturated copy of the media behind it, with a lit top edge. For controls floating over photos or video only. Over bright media, a 35% dark layer under the glass keeps the white label legible; the Dim toggle shows the difference.
ArrangementAlert-style groups: up to three buttons. In a row the default is trailing and Cancel leading. In a stack the default is on top and Cancel at the bottom. All buttons share one height; in a row they share one width. Style ranks the choices, not size.

Specs

What Apple states, and what we chose. Apple states: a hit region of at least 44x44 pt (60x60 in visionOS), 17 pt default and 11 pt minimum text on iOS, 4.5:1 text contrast (3:1 for bold or 18 pt), visionOS heights of 28, 32, 44, 52 and 64 pt, and one or two prominent buttons per view. Apple states no corner radii and no push-button heights for iOS or macOS, so the radii, paddings, fills, press scale and glass values below are ours, in the style of Apple, not Apple’s numbers. The blue is darker than system blue because white on #0088FF is under 4.5:1.

Behavior

PressScales to 96% and darkens 12% on :active, in 120ms. Plain buttons also take a tint fill. A custom button needs a pressed state; without one it feels unresponsive.
DisabledDimmed label, light fill, not focusable, no press. Contrast is not required for disabled controls.
ToggleClick or Space/Enter flips aria-pressed; fill, icon and label follow it.
HierarchyOne or two prominent buttons per view. The page shows many only because it is a catalog. In the alert pair only Save is prominent.
DestructiveRed label on gray, tinted and plain; a red fill only when it is the single prominent action. In the stack it is gray with red text in the middle, so Save Draft stays the default.
GlassUse sparingly, on a few controls over media, not for content. With Reduce transparency set the fill turns opaque and the blur is dropped.
MotionOnly the press and toggle transitions animate. They stop with reduced motion; the pressed look stays.
Not builtiOS 26’s springy glass morphing, the haptic tap, and the in-button activity indicator (see Async Buttons).

Accessibility

KeyDoes
TabMoves between buttons; each shows a 3px ring with a 2px gap in both themes.
Space EnterActivates a button, or flips a toggle.
  • All controls are native <button>. Icon-only buttons carry aria-label; toggles carry aria-pressed. The alert cards are role="group" named by their titles.
  • The Rules check below measures: every touch control is 44px or taller (compact faces sit inside a 44px button), text is at least 11px, solid-background text meets 4.5:1 in both themes, a :active rule exists, and animation has a reduced-motion rule.
  • It cannot measure the glass buttons: text over a blur or gradient is marked for review. The white label passes only with the 35% layer; check it on your own media.
  • It also cannot judge wording (verb first, title case, one or two words), whether Cancel and default sit where the HIG puts them, whether red is used for the right action, or whether an icon is familiar. Those need a person.
